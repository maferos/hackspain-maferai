Uncap and pipette hand

The parabolic cradle gripper holds the bottle; the iris clamp swings down, unscrews the cap and lifts it straight off; the micropipette swings in over the neck, draws, and the clamp screws the cap back.

    Layout

    Gripper. Tool axis 21 mm under the cap seat on every bottle, flange 118 mm behind the jaws, nothing but the jaws and the beam within ±16 mm of the axis. The cradles close to (r² + p²)/2p and hold the bottle on four lines; their wings interleave.

    Iris clamp. Six blades on a 55 mm pivot circle, a cam ring and a planetary train under one motor: the sun closes the iris until the blades meet the cap, then the clutch lets the whole housing turn and the thread walks the cap up 2.75 mm a turn. The clamp hangs from a swing arm hinged 98 mm behind the bottle axis and 8 mm over the iris plane, on two cheeks bolted to the gripper housing. The arm's post doubles as the column of the clamp's own 20 mm axis: once the thread lets go the clamp pulls the cap straight up, clear of the neck, before it swings away, and the same axis follows the cap up the thread while it unscrews. Swung up, the post and the housing lie 3.5 mm over the wrist.

    Micropipette. Held by two rings in a swing frame on a vertical pin at (30, 90) mm, on the carriage of a slide beside the bottle. It swings 80° out to the arm's side while the clamp works, swings back in over the neck once the clamp is up, seats on a cone, and only then does the slide take it down the neck. Its plunger has its own actuator on the frame.
