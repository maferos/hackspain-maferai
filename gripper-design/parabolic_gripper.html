Readout

Why

One cradle for every bottle. A parabola y = v − x²/2p with p = 8 mm holds any cylinder of radius r > p on two symmetric lines at x = ±√(r² − p²), always at height y = p. The contact walks outwards as the bottle grows, so the same pair cradles the 10 ml and the 100 ml, and the pair centres the bottle. Two lines per jaw, four in all: the wedge is what lets the hand take the unscrewing torque.

Compact about its axis. The 2F-85 hangs 37.5 mm below its axis and rises 13.5 mm above it; between the table and the clamp's housing that leaves no room under a 57.5 mm cap seat, so the 10, 20 and 30 ml bottles could not be picked. Here nothing but the jaws and the beam sits around the axis, ±16 mm: the axis goes 21 mm under the cap seat on every bottle, and the drive lives behind the jaws where there is room. Toggle the ghost on the 10 ml to see the difference.

Interleaved wings. Closed on anything under Ø42 mm the cradles' wings cross the centre plane (by 20 mm on the 10 ml). The bench never saw it because jaw and jaw do not collide in MuJoCo; a real pair would clash. So beyond |x| = 12 mm each cradle is a comb: the left jaw's wings are the two outer bands, the right jaw's the middle one, and they pass through each other with 1 mm to spare. The two lines of contact are unchanged.
