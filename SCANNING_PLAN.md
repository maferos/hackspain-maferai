# The first scanning pass: building the bench memory

A plan, not an implementation. It says what the robot's first pass over the
bench does, what it writes down, and how that record survives the bench
changing under it.

Owners: Nacho, Martí (vision), with the rail scene from Eki, Eloi. See
`AGENTS.md`.

> **Status, 2026-09-19.** Gap 1 below, "there is no memory", is closed, but not
> by the new file this plan proposed. The propose-then-confirm pass now lives
> inside the harness's own generator, `harness/build_lookup_table.py`, which
> writes `harness/lookup_table.json` --- the fixed cameras propose, the wrist
> camera confirms from 0.30 m, and both feed the same decoder. Gaps 2 (the arm
> is not in the loop: the wrist camera is still a mocap mount, not the UR10e)
> and 3 (no update policy: every pass rebuilds the table from scratch) are
> open, and so is everything under "The memory format" and "The merge rules".

## The goal in one line

```
general camera --detector--> boxes --ray x plane--> where things stand
                                                         |
                                        the arm drives the wrist camera there
                                                         |
                                     ArUco ring --> which sample stands there
                                                         |
                                                    bench_map.json
                                          sample_id -> compound + 3D position
```

One pass and the lab has a table of *what* is on the bench and *where*. Run it
again and the table follows the bench: a flask that moved moves, a flask that
appears is added, a flask that is hidden behind a neighbour is not forgotten.

## What already exists

The hard half is built and measured. Nothing below needs rebuilding.

| Piece | What it does | Measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `labvision/scene.py` `locate()` | detector box -> metres on the bench, by intersecting the pixel's ray with the worktop plane | 0.00 mm of geometric error from an exact box; a pixel is worth 5.5 mm of bench at 1080p |
| `labvision/perception.py` `propose()` | every box of the fixed camera -> merged proposals on the bench | 10 mm median / 27 mm p90 position |
| `labvision/perception.py` `confirm()` | a wrist frame -> the ArUco ring read near the proposal, naming the sample | 96 % named, 0 misnamed over 77 bottles |
| `labvision/perception.py` `refine_marker()` | the most frontal marker of the ring -> the bottle's true axis | 0.1 mm median / 0.5 mm p90 |
| `labvision/world.py` | `PerceivedBottle`, the record the rest of the lab consumes | — |
| `simulation/scripts/rail_kinematics.py` | top-down IK, `reach()` over the rail, `label_view()` | 187/187 vessels reachable from the rail |
| `barcodes/lookup_table.json` | the catalogue: 200 samples, compound, CAS, ml, `vessel_class`, `marker_id` | — |

`computer-vision/scripts/propose_confirm.py` already chains propose -> confirm
end to end and scores it.

## What is missing --- three gaps

**1. There is no memory.** `propose_confirm.py` is a *bench*: it scores against
the simulator's truth and writes a report. Nothing persists and nothing
accumulates between passes. `lookup_table.json` is the catalogue --- identity
--- not the state of the bench, and the two must not be merged: the catalogue
is deterministic build output, regenerated by `python -m labvision.registry`,
and a position written into it would be destroyed by the next regeneration.

**2. The robot is not in the loop.** `propose_confirm.py` runs on
`minihannover_scene.xml` and flies a *mocap* camera that teleports to each
proposal. The rail scene has a real UR10e with an eye-in-hand camera and a
`reach()` that can fail. That changes the pass: the route has to be planned
along the rail, and some vessels will not be reachable from any station.

**3. There is no update policy.** What to do when a known flask has moved, when
a new one appears, when a known one is not seen at all.

## The scene

`simulation/models/minihannover_rail_scene.xml`, as committed:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Worktop | centre (-1.5, -0.4), half extents (3.0, 1.0), top **z = 0.90** |
| `general` camera | (-1.5, -2.9, 3), fovy 60.44, 1920x1080, looking at the bench from -Y |
| `wrist` camera | eye-in-hand on the arm, fovy 60.44, 1920x1080 |
| Vessels | 12 open flasks, x from -3.88 to -0.14, y from -0.81 to -0.28, all on the worktop |

All twelve carry the ArUco ring (`DICT_4X4_250`, eight markers, one every 45
degrees) and all twelve are in the `general` camera's field of view.

## The pass, step by step

```
[1] Park the arm         carriage to one end, arm folded back towards +Y
        |                 -- otherwise the arm occludes bottles in the general frame
[2] General camera       render `general` at 1920x1080
        |
[3] Detector             YOLO-World with the bottle prompts (threshold 0.11);
        |                 --weights swaps in the fine-tuned YOLO26n when it lands
[4] Worktop filter       drop boxes whose base does not land on the worktop
        |
[5] propose()            ray x plane at z = 0.90  ->  N positions, no identity yet
        |
[6] Order the route      proposals sorted by X, so the carriage sweeps the rail
        |                 once in one direction rather than going back and forth
[7] Per proposal:
        |-- ask the memory: is anything known within 5 cm?
        |      yes -> its vessel_class is known -> aim at its REAL ring height
        |      no  -> aim 0.03 m above the worktop (covers all five sizes)
        |-- for each bearing in rail_kinematics.BEARINGS:
        |      reach() the eih site to 0.30 m standoff, 8 degrees of elevation
        |      IK fails        -> next bearing
        |      render wrist -> confirm() -> read a ring?
        |      read nothing    -> next bearing
        |      read a ring     -> stop
        `-- perceived(proposal, confirmation)
[8] one_per_sample()     two proposals on one flask collapse to one record
[9] Merge into memory    <- the new step
[10] Write               bench_map.json, a report, and frames on request
```

**Why 0.03 m for an unknown vessel.** The rings sit 17 / 23 / 27 / 32 / 41 mm
above the base across the five flask sizes. At 0.30 m of standoff a 60.44
degree frame covers 0.35 m vertically, so one fixed aim at 28 mm puts all five
comfortably in shot. Once a flask is named the memory knows its
`vessel_class`, and `perception.ring_geometry()` gives the exact height --- so
**the second pass aims better than the first**. That is the adaptive behaviour,
showing up in the aiming and not only in the bookkeeping.

**Why the bearings are tried blind.** The pipeline never asks the simulator
whether a view is blocked. It looks, reads nothing, and tries the next bearing
--- which is what an arm in a real lab has to do.

## The memory format

A new file, versioned, separate from the catalogue: `bench_map.json`.

```jsonc
{
  "version": 1,
  "scene": "simulation/models/minihannover_rail_scene.xml",
  "frame": "world metres, bench top z = 0.90",
  "scans": [{ "id": 0, "at": "...", "proposals": 14, "named": 12 }],
  "entries": {
    "SMP-0011": {
      // identity, copied from the catalogue when the ring is read
      "marker_id": 10, "barcode": "2008070695988",
      "material": "Geraniol", "cas": "106-24-1",
      "container_ml": 10.0, "vessel_class": "flask_10ml", "phase": "liquid",
      // location, which is what this file adds
      "position": [-1.5344, -0.5048, 0.90],
      "refined": true,            // from the ring, not from the detector box
      "ring_height": 0.0173,      // so the next pass aims straight at it
      // provenance
      "confidence": 0.71, "votes": 3, "bearing": [0.0, 1.0],
      "first_seen": 0, "last_seen": 0, "sightings": 1, "status": "present"
    },
    "UNK-0001": {                 // proposed, never named
      "position": [-2.11, -0.63, 0.90], "refined": false,
      "status": "unidentified", "attempts": 5, "last_seen": 0
    }
  }
}
```

Two kinds of entry on purpose: **the robot needs to know something stands there
even when it does not know what it is** --- so as not to hit it, and so as to
come back and try again.

Identity is always `sample_id`, resolved from `marker_id`. The ArUco ring is
the primary key; the position is an attribute of it, never the other way round.

## The merge rules --- the adaptive core

| Situation | What the memory does |
| --- | --- |
| Named, already known, within 5 cm of where it was | update the position, `sightings++`, `last_seen` |
| Named, already known, **far** from where it was | it has been moved: replace the position, flag `moved` |
| **Named, not in memory** | **insert** --- the new flask that appeared on the bench |
| Unnamed, near a known entry | refresh `last_seen`; do not rename it and do not move it |
| Unnamed, far from everything | a new `UNK-*` entry, to be retried |
| Known, not seen, and its part of the bench was scanned | `status: "missing"` --- **not deleted** |

That last row is the one that matters. A flask hidden behind a neighbour is not
a flask that left. Deleting on a single miss is how a memory loses things it
already knew; degrading to `missing` lets the next pass recover it by itself.

## What has to be fixed first

Four concrete things found in the repo while planning this. None is serious;
all are real.

**a) The worktop filter carries the other scene's constants.**
`scripts/perfumery_eval.py` has `WORKTOP_HALF = (3.0, 0.75)` and assumes a
bench centred on the origin. The rail scene uses the *open* bench: centre
(-1.5, -0.4), half extents (3.0, 1.0), top 0.90. With today's constants
`SMP-0199` (y = -0.811) falls outside the filter and would be dropped before it
was ever proposed. The filter has to take the worktop as a parameter instead of
inheriting one scene's.

**b) `rail_kinematics.read_label()` reads the simulator's truth.** It calls
`line_of_sight()`, which asks MuJoCo which geom a ray hits first, and it takes
a `Bottle` recovered from geom names. A scanning robot knows neither. The
honest half --- pose the arm so the eye-in-hand camera looks at an arbitrary
world point from a given bearing --- should be factored out, and a blocked view
discovered by reading nothing, as `propose_confirm.py` already does.

**c) The arm appears in the general camera's frame.** The gantry sits behind
the glassware from the `general` camera's point of view, but an extended arm
does not. Step [1], parking before looking, is not cosmetic.

**d) Intrinsics should come from the scene.** Do not hardcode
`gopro_intrinsics()`: the rail scene's eye-in-hand camera is a GigE
machine-vision camera, not a GoPro. `Intrinsics.from_fov(w, h,
fovy_deg=model.cam_fovy[i])` reads the field of view MuJoCo compiled and is
right for both cameras.

## How it is verified

The rail scene's twelve flasks have known truth, so the first pass is scored
the way `propose_confirm.py` scores: how many proposed, how many named right,
how many named wrong, position error before and after the ring refines it.

**Target: 12/12 named, 0 wrong, under 1 mm after refinement.**

And the test that the memory is actually adaptive, which is the point of the
whole thing: run pass 1, then move one flask and add another by writing `qpos`,
then run pass 2 --- and check that the moved one moves, the new one is
inserted, and none of the other ten is duplicated or lost.

## Environment

The pass needs MuJoCo *and* OpenCV *and* Ultralytics in one interpreter, which
no environment in the repo currently provides: `simulation/.venv` has MuJoCo
without OpenCV, and the environment matching `computer-vision/requirements.txt`
has OpenCV and Ultralytics without MuJoCo. Adding `mujoco` to the vision
environment is the cheap fix --- about 60 MB --- and it also makes
`propose_confirm.py`, which lives in `computer-vision/` and imports MuJoCo,
runnable where it sits.
