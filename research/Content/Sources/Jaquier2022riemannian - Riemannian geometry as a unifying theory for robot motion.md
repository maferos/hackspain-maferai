> [!abstract] One-sentence summary
> This blue sky paper argues that geodesic synergies on the robot configuration manifold, with the mass-inertia matrix as metric, can unify energy-efficient robot motion generation and perception-action coupling, backed only by an ARMAR-6 arm proof of concept.

## Abstract

Riemannian geometry is a mathematical field which has been the cornerstone of revolutionary scientific discoveries such as the theory of general relativity. Despite early uses in robot design and recent applications for exploiting data with specific geometries, it mostly remains overlooked in robotics. With this blue sky paper, we argue that Riemannian geometry provides the most suitable tools to analyze and generate well-coordinated, energy-efficient motions of robots with many degrees of freedom. Via preliminary solutions and novel research directions, we discuss how Riemannian geometry may be leveraged to design and combine physically-meaningful synergies for robotics, and how this theory also opens the door to coupling motion synergies with perceptual inputs. (arXiv)

## 📖 Glossary

- **Riemannian manifold** — Smooth manifold equipped with a smoothly-varying inner product on its tangent spaces.
- **Metric tensor** — Symmetric positive-definite matrix G(x) representing the Riemannian metric in local coordinates.
- **Kinetic-energy metric** — Riemannian metric whose tensor equals the mass-inertia matrix at each joint configuration.
- **Geodesic** — Minimum-energy, minimum-length, constant-velocity curve generalizing straight lines to manifolds.
- **Geodesic synergy** — Joint coordination given by a geodesic from a chosen initial configuration and velocity.
- **Parallel transport** — Operation moving a tangent vector between tangent spaces of different points on a manifold.
- **Principal geodesic analysis (PGA)** — Riemannian dimensionality reduction method, the manifold counterpart of PCA.
- **Christoffel symbols** — Terms derived from metric derivatives that capture Coriolis effects in the geodesic equation.

## ❓ Open questions

- How can relevant geodesic synergies be extracted from human motion data and selected for a given robot?
- How can human geodesic synergies be transferred to non-anthropomorphic robots with different configuration manifolds?
- How should the Riemannian metric be shaped to handle loads, contacts and external perturbations while keeping motions safe?
- What form would Riemannian optimal controllers built on geodesic synergies take?
- How can robot perception spaces be endowed with Riemannian metrics, and how can mappings to the configuration manifold be learned?
- Do geodesic synergies outperform PCA-based synergies quantitatively in energy use, naturalness or task success on real robots?
- How do geodesic synergies behave under gravity, which the geodesic equation excludes?

## 📝 Notes on reading

Read the arXiv preprint 2209.15539v1 (8 pages), a blue sky position paper. It contains no quantitative experiments: the only evidence is illustrative (Fig. 1, a 2-DoF planar robot; Fig. 2, the right arm of ARMAR-6 with four weight combinations of two synergies, e.g. w1 = 1.2, w2 = −0.5 and w1 = −0.5, w2 = 1.0). The comparison with PCA-based linear synergies is given only as an external video (youtu.be/XblzcKRRITE), not in the paper. Fig. 1 legend glyphs (colors of metric ellipsoids, geodesic and Euclidean paths) were lost in extraction; Fig. 1 caption states energy is reflected by ellipsoid volume proportional to det(G). Equation (1) and the Christoffel symbol formula are partly garbled in the cached text and were described rather than claimed. Note the geodesic equation assumes no gravity and no external forces, so the minimum-energy property applies to passive motion only; the paper does not discuss gravity compensation explicitly.
