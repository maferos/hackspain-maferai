> [!abstract] One-sentence summary
> The paper shows that Gaussian process priors generated by white-noise-driven stochastic differential equations have exactly sparse inverse kernel matrices, which makes batch continuous-time trajectory estimation linear in trajectory length with constant-time queries at any time of interest.

## Abstract

In this paper, we revisit batch state estimation through the lens of Gaussian process (GP) regression. We consider continuous-discrete estimation problems wherein a trajectory is viewed as a one-dimensional GP, with time as the independent variable. Our continuous-time prior can be defined by any nonlinear, time-varying stochastic differential equation driven by white noise; this allows the possibility of smoothing our trajectory estimates using a variety of vehicle dynamics models (e.g., `constant-velocity'). We show that this class of prior results in an inverse kernel matrix (i.e., covariance matrix between all pairs of measurement times) that is exactly sparse (block-tridiagonal) and that this can be exploited to carry out GP regression (and interpolation) very efficiently. When the prior is based on a linear, time-varying stochastic differential equation and the measurement model is also linear, this GP approach is equivalent to classical, discrete-time smoothing (at the measurement times); when a nonlinearity is present, we iterate over the whole trajectory to maximize accuracy. We test the approach experimentally on a simultaneous trajectory estimation and mapping problem using a mobile robot dataset. (arXiv)

## 🧠 Key ideas (atomic)

- Trajectory estimation for mobile robots has been formulated almost exclusively in discrete time despite continuous-time techniques being available since the 1960s. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:cited` p. 1 ^anderson2014batch-001
- For a scanning-while-moving sensor, a discrete-time approach without a motion prior can fail to find a unique solution. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 2 ^anderson2014batch-002
- The authors represent the robot trajectory nonparametrically as a one-dimensional Gaussian process with time as the independent variable. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 2 ^anderson2014batch-003
- The earlier GP regression approach of Tong et al. is very general but expensive because it inverts a large, dense kernel matrix. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:cited` p. 2 ^anderson2014batch-004
- The paper considers a class of GP priors generated by nonlinear, time-varying stochastic differential equations driven by white noise. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 2 ^anderson2014batch-005
- Many commonly used covariance functions, such as the Matérn class, can be exactly or approximately represented as linear SDEs. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:cited` p. 2 ^anderson2014batch-006
- The authors explain that the sparsity arises because the estimated state is Markovian for this class of GPs. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 2 ^anderson2014batch-007
- The authors introduce simultaneous trajectory estimation and mapping (STEAM), a SLAM generalization replacing chains of discrete poses with Markovian trajectories. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 2 ^anderson2014batch-008
- The paper extends an earlier conference paper from linear to nonlinear time-varying SDE priors without losing the linear case's sparsity. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 3 ^anderson2014batch-009
- Each iteration solves a linear system for a perturbation to the trajectory guess, effectively performing Gauss-Newton optimization over the whole trajectory. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 3 ^anderson2014batch-010
- Without the sparse structure, the general GP approach has a cost cubic in the number of measurement times, which is quite expensive. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 4 ^anderson2014batch-011
- The kernel matrix admits a lower-diagonal-upper factorization whose outer factor is the lower-triangular lifted transition matrix. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 5 ^anderson2014batch-012
- For GP priors from linear, time-varying SDEs, the inverse kernel matrix is exactly sparse, specifically block-tridiagonal. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 5 ^anderson2014batch-013
- Using the block-tridiagonal prior, each iteration's linear system can be solved in O(N) time with a sparse Cholesky solver. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 5 ^anderson2014batch-014
- Although the prior is continuous-time, the MAP problem needs only N + 1 smoothing terms, one per consecutive measurement-time pair plus one initial. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 5 ^anderson2014batch-015
- Under the constant-velocity prior, position alone is not Markovian, whereas the stacked position-velocity state is. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 6 ^anderson2014batch-016
- Marginalizing out the velocity variables fills in the inverse kernel matrix, thereby destroying its sparsity. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 6 ^anderson2014batch-017
- The interpolated state at a query time is a linear combination of terms from only the two bounding measurement times. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 6 ^anderson2014batch-018
- Querying the trajectory at a single time of interest has O(1) complexity under this class of sparse priors. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 6 ^anderson2014batch-019
- Estimating the state only at selected keytimes further reduces computational cost with some loss of accuracy. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:cited` p. 7 ^anderson2014batch-020
- With keytime interpolation, the measurement term becomes block-tridiagonal, but solve complexity is unaffected because the prior term is also block-tridiagonal. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 7 ^anderson2014batch-021
- The authors note there is no principled method to choose keytime spacing that guarantees a bound on accuracy loss. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 7 ^anderson2014batch-022
- For nonlinear priors, the SDE is linearized about a continuous-time operating point, recovering the familiar linear time-varying SDE form. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 7 ^anderson2014batch-023
- With a nonlinear prior, the prior moments must be recalculated at every iteration because the linearization point is updated. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 8 ^anderson2014batch-024
- When no analytical transition matrix is available, it is computed numerically by integrating the normalized fundamental matrix. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 8 ^anderson2014batch-025
- A nonlinear process model adds numerical-integration cost, but complexity remains linear in trajectory length at each iteration. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 8 ^anderson2014batch-026
- With the sparse prior, total hyperparameter training complexity is bottlenecked at quadratic cost in the number of observations. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 9 ^anderson2014batch-027
- Linear-time hyperparameter training is achievable only by ignoring the additive noise on the ground-truth observations. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 9 ^anderson2014batch-028
- Ignoring that noise makes the trained hyperparameters give an underconfident prior, whose degree depends on the ignored noise magnitude. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 9 ^anderson2014batch-029
- Exploiting sparsity, the full algorithm with N measurement times plus J query times has O(N + J) complexity. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 10 ^anderson2014batch-030
- The linear constant-velocity prior places white noise on acceleration, with a state stacking planar pose with pose rate. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 10 ^anderson2014batch-031
- The nonlinear constant-velocity prior applies white noise to acceleration in the robot body frame, since the main acceleration source is robot-oriented. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 10 ^anderson2014batch-032
- The experiment uses laser range/bearing measurements to landmarks plus wheel odometry giving longitudinal and rotational speeds. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 11 ^anderson2014batch-033
- The STEAM solve exploits the block-tridiagonal trajectory block through sparse Cholesky decomposition, costing cubic in landmarks but linear in measurement times. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 11 ^anderson2014batch-034
- The experiment uses the Tong et al. dataset: a laser-equipped mobile robot driving indoors among 17 plastic-tube landmarks. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 11 ^anderson2014batch-035
- In this dataset, both the odometry measurements and the landmark measurements are provided at a rate of 1Hz. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 11 ^anderson2014batch-036
- After estimator convergence, additional trajectory queries are computed at a rate of 10Hz for evaluation. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-037
- Ground-truth for the robot trajectory and landmark positions comes from a Vicon motion capture system. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-038
- Three estimators were compared: Tong's GP-Pose-Dense, a non-sparse GP-Traj-Dense, and the sparsity-exploiting GP-Traj-Sparse in LTI and NTV variants. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-039
- In the experiment, QC was modelled as a diagonal matrix trained via log marginal likelihood with ground-truth measurements. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-040
- Although the estimators differed in degrees of freedom and state types, their overall accuracies were similar on this dataset. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-041
- Qualitatively, the GP-Traj-Sparse-NTV trajectory more accurately matched the local shape of the ground-truth on many occasions. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-042
- The plotted 3σ covariance envelope shows that the GP-Traj-Sparse-NTV estimate tends to be more uncertain. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-043
- All algorithms were implemented in Matlab on a 2.4GHz i7 processor with 8GB of 1333MHz DDR3 RAM. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-044
- GP-Traj-Dense is much slower than GP-Pose-Dense because reintroducing velocity doubles the number of trajectory variables. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-045
- For the GP-Traj-Sparse methods, kernel matrix construction time was linear in the number of estimated states. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-046
- As predicted, GP-Traj-Sparse optimization time per iteration was also linear in the number of estimated states. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-047
- For the GP-Traj-Sparse methods, interpolation time per additional query was constant regardless of the state size. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-048
- Total computation time for the GP-Traj-Sparse methods was also linear, as shown in the timing figure. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-049
- GP-Traj-Sparse-NTV needs more kernel construction time than the LTI variant, due to linearizing plus numerically integrating the prior moments. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 12 ^anderson2014batch-050
- The GP-Traj-Sparse implementations converged in fewer iterations, which the authors attribute to greater numerical stability from directly constructing the inverse kernel matrix. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 13 ^anderson2014batch-051
- The GP-Traj-Sparse approaches clearly outperform the other algorithms in terms of computational cost on this dataset. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 13 ^anderson2014batch-052
- To expose the nonlinear prior's benefit, the authors increased problem nonlinearity by reducing the range measurement frequency. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:reported` p. 13 ^anderson2014batch-053
- As the interval between range measurements increased, GP-Traj-Sparse-NTV produced more accurate continuous-time pose estimates than GP-Traj-Sparse-LTI. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 13 ^anderson2014batch-054
- When 1 Hz odometry is available, the difference between the two estimators' rotation estimates is small. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 13 ^anderson2014batch-055
- Without odometry measurements, the advantage of the nonlinear prior is prominent in the rotational estimate. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 13 ^anderson2014batch-056
- With an interval of 7 seconds between range measurements, the GP-Traj-Sparse-NTV estimate matches the ground-truth more closely. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:measured` p. 13 ^anderson2014batch-057
- The authors argue that treating odometry as inputs to the prior mean conflates trajectory-prior uncertainty with odometry noise. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 13 ^anderson2014batch-058
- Passing white noise through a specific linear SDE makes position follow a Matérn covariance with p = 1. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:computed` p. 15 ^anderson2014batch-059
- As future work, the authors plan to incorporate robot dynamics, kinematics plus Newtonian mechanics, into the GP priors. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 15 ^anderson2014batch-060
- The authors hope the paper deepens the connection between classical state estimation theory and recent machine learning methods. (Anderson et al., 2014) `ev:asserted` p. 15 ^anderson2014batch-061

## 📖 Glossary

- **STEAM** — Simultaneous trajectory estimation and mapping: SLAM with a continuous-time Markovian trajectory prior.
- **Inverse kernel matrix** — Inverse of the prior covariance between all pairs of estimation times.
- **Block-tridiagonal** — Matrix whose only non-zero blocks lie on the main diagonal and its two neighbours.
- **LTV SDE** — Linear, time-varying stochastic differential equation driven by white noise.
- **NTV SDE** — Nonlinear, time-varying stochastic differential equation, linearized about an operating point here.
- **Constant-velocity prior** — Motion prior with white noise on acceleration; the Markovian state stacks pose and velocity.
- **Lifted transition matrix** — Lower-triangular matrix of transition matrices between all pairs of measurement times.
- **Keytimes** — Reduced set of times where the state is estimated; measurements elsewhere are interpolated.
- **Normalized fundamental matrix** — Solution of the homogeneous linearized dynamics, used to compute transition matrices numerically.

## ❓ Open questions

- How should keytime spacing be chosen so that the loss of accuracy from state reduction is bounded?
- Can hyperparameter training reach linear cost without ignoring ground-truth measurement noise and making the prior underconfident?
- How much does a full dynamics prior (kinematics plus Newtonian mechanics) improve accuracy over the constant-velocity priors?
- How does the approach scale when integrated with modern incremental solvers on large, online STEAM problems?
- Does the accuracy advantage of the nonlinear prior hold on datasets other than the single indoor 17-landmark dataset?

## Suggested new concepts

- Exactly sparse Gaussian process priors — the core idea linking SDE-generated GP priors to block-tridiagonal inverse kernels, reused across continuous-time estimation work.
- Simultaneous trajectory estimation and mapping (STEAM) — a named generalization of SLAM with continuous-time trajectory priors.
- Continuous-time trajectory estimation — the broader family (GP-based and spline-based) this paper belongs to and contrasts with.
- GP interpolation for trajectory queries — constant-time querying of a continuous-time estimate at arbitrary times.
