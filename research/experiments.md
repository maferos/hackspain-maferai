## 0. Three corrections to the shortlist

The shortlist is sound generic advice. Three project-specific facts reorder it.

### 0.1 There is no MDP yet

`simulation/models/minihannover_scene.xml` compiles with **`nu = 0`** — no arm, no
actuator, no gripper, no sites, no sensors. 962 geoms of set dressing and 7 free-floating
amber bottles. The only commandable robot anywhere in the repository is AutoBio's
(`nu = 7`, ALOHA / UR5e), pinned to **MuJoCo 3.3.0** and Linux-only.

Choosing Isaac Lab means not inheriting AutoBio's robot, **nor its scripted experts, nor
its `task.check()` success oracle**. Those have to be built. That is the critical path —
not model selection. Everything below is gated on §1.

### 0.2 Isaac Lab is the right call, including for a reason easy to miss

`simulation/tools/convert_glb.py` discards UVs and textures; `assets/README.md` states
that labels render white. So the **200 barcode-labelled GLBs cannot appear in a MuJoCo
scene at all**. USD import preserves them.

Isaac Lab is therefore a *prerequisite* for any experiment using the project's most
distinctive asset, not merely a performance preference. Separately, Orbit
(`Content/Sources/Mittal2023orbit - ...md`) reports **50,000–75,000 FPS at 2048 envs**,
which is what promotes RL from last place.

### 0.3 The ACT-vs-Diffusion comparison has already been run, on Isaac Sim, on lab tasks

`Content/Sources/Li2025labutopia - LabUtopia High-Fidelity Simulation and Hierarchical.md`
benchmarks ACT, Diffusion Policy and π0 on Franka Panda lab manipulation in Isaac Sim.

| Task | ACT | Diffusion Policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level-2 Heater Beaker | **86.7 %** | 25.0 % |
| Level-2 Operate Drawer | **73.3 %** | 10.0 % |
| Level-2 Stir with GlassRod | **55.0 %** | 10.0 % |
| Level-3 Transport Beaker, ID → OOD | held 65.0–96.7 % | 67.5 → **15.0 %** |

Diffusion Policy *"frequently stalled, e.g. pausing during Heater Beaker's final action
until timeout."* And, verbatim: *"Fine-tuned pretrained VLA models did not consistently
outperform models trained from scratch on Level-3 tasks."*

This contradicts two of the shortlist's premises in exactly this domain: that Diffusion
Policy is the more robust choice under randomization, and that large pretrained VLAs
generalize best. Plain Diffusion Policy stays in the plan as a **control**, not as a step up.

### 0.4 Revised order of practicality for this project

| Rank | Family | Why the change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **ACT** | Unchanged, and now with direct Isaac-Sim lab evidence (LabUtopia) including the best OOD retention |
| 2 | **Equivariant Diffusion Policy** | Absent from the original shortlist; it is the vault's actual edge and the bottles are best-case geometry |
| 3 | **RL refinement** | Up from #5 — Isaac Lab's parallel envs are what make it cheap |
| 4 | **SmolVLA** | Retained for language/identity conditioning only; note §5.2 |
| 5 | **Plain Diffusion Policy** | Demoted to control arm for B4 / I2 |
| 6 | **π0.5 / GR00T / X-VLA** | Demoted: the ready-made LIBERO/RoboCasa fine-tunes are robosuite/MuJoCo, and LabUtopia found fine-tuned VLAs did not consistently win |
| 7 | **World models** | Agreed — do not start |

## 1. P0 — the gate (a prerequisite, not an experiment)

Isaac Lab + stock Franka Panda + the six agrochemical vessels as USD.

**Task `BottleToBalance`.** Grasp the named bottle; place it on the analytical balance pan.

- **Expert**: Isaac Lab `DifferentialIKController`.
- **Success**: bottle centroid within pan radius, **held continuously for 2 s** — LabUtopia's
  definition, adopted verbatim so the numbers are comparable.
- **Randomization**: **15 cm × 15 cm** placement region — also LabUtopia's, same reason.
- **Cameras**: `general` + `wrist`, both already mounted and pinned to `labvision`'s
  `default_camera()` — GoPro Linear, 1080p, `fovy` 60.44. Port both to the Isaac Lab scene
  unchanged so CV's measurements carry over. `wrist` currently rides a mocap body; §1 is what
  turns it into an actual wrist camera.

**Why this task.** SO(2)-symmetric cylinders on a plane (equivariance applies); two valid
grasp approaches (multimodality applies); 200 barcode identities (language applies); and a
success oracle that needs no new physics.

**Exit criterion: 200 scripted demonstrations at ≥ 95 % expert success.**

> **Known blocker.** `simulation/assets/balance/balance.xml` is visual meshes
> (`contype=0, density=0`) plus one 6 kg collision box — **no joint, no pan, no weight
> sensor**. Placement is checkable; *weighing is not*, until a force sensor is authored.
> Any "weigh N grams" task is therefore out of scope until that lands.

## 2. Baseline experiments

| ID | Design | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B1** | ACT, 50 demos, fixed bottle pose | Pipeline works end to end. Reference cost: ACT paper reports ~5 h on a single 11 GB RTX 2080 Ti, ~0.01 s inference |
| **B2** | ACT × demos {10, 25, 50, 100, 200} × {fixed, 15×15 cm} | The data-efficiency curve that doc 03 §8.4 explicitly asks for ("success vs number of demonstrations") |
| **B3** | Diffusion Policy, same grid as B2 | ACT vs DP under identical data. Prediction from LabUtopia: ACT ≥ DP, and DP may stall |
| **B4** | Demos deliberately generated with **two grasp approaches** (left and right of the bottle) | The *only* condition in which DP should beat ACT |

**B4 is the one that earns its place.** It converts the stated rationale for Diffusion
Policy — that it handles multimodal demonstrations where ACT averages them into a bad
middle path — into a falsifiable claim rather than an assumption. Register the prediction
before running it.

Training budgets to copy from LabUtopia: ACT and DP **200 epochs, AdamW, single RTX 4090**,
LR 1e-4, batch 128 (ACT) / 64 (DP), action chunk 60. π0 for comparison: 30,000 steps on
8 × A800 80 GB, chunk 8.

## 3. Innovative experiments

### I1 — Action-space geometry ablation

*Highest value per GPU-hour. Run this first.*

Same ACT, same data, same seeds; vary **only** how the action chunk encodes rotation:

| Arm | Representation |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | Joint deltas `Δq` |
| (b) | End-effector pose deltas with **Euler** rotation (what most OXE-style datasets ship) |
| (c) | **6D rotation + relative poses** `{T_t⁻¹ T_{t+k}}` converted with `Log` |

`02_optimizacion_y_algoritmos.md` §D.5 gives the reason (c) should win: accumulating
twists across a chunk is only first-order correct, because `Exp(a)Exp(b) ≠ Exp(a+b)` (BCH).
§D.6 recommends (c) outright. The strongest empirical prior in the vault is UMI
(`Content/Sources/Chi2024universal - ...md`): relative action **20/20**, delta baseline
**16/20**, absolute baseline **5/20**.

Three runs, one variable. It tests the vault's central design claim at minimal cost.

### I2 — Equivariance vs augmentation under demonstration scarcity

*The headline result.*

Four arms × demos {10, 25, 50, 100}:

1. Diffusion Policy
2. Diffusion Policy **+ rotation augmentation**
3. Equivariant Diffusion Policy (SO(2))
4. EquiBot (SIM(3)) — optional fourth arm

**The augmentation arm is mandatory.** Without it the result is uninteresting, because
"equivariance beats a baseline that never saw rotations" is trivial. Prior from
`Content/Sources/Wang2024equivariant - Equivariant Diffusion Policy.md`: random-rotation
augmentation on an unconstrained CNN averaged **53.3 vs 63.9** for the equivariant model —
augmentation is *not* a substitute.

Supporting priors, all in-vault:

| Source | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| `Wang2024equivariant` | 200 demos outperformed **all baselines trained with 1000 demos**; +21.9 % avg at 100 demos; removing equivariance costs **−17.6**, removing voxel input only −10.3 |
| `Yang2024equibot` | On Robomimic Can/Square, DP "drops dramatically from 100 to 25 demos" while EquiBot holds |
| `Tie2024et` (ET-SEED) | Trajectory-level SE(3); real tasks from **20 teleop demos** |
| `Ryu2023diffusion` (Diffusion-EDFs) | Bi-equivariant; **5–10 demos, 20–45 min on one RTX 3090** |

The agrochemical bottles are SO(2)-symmetric cylinders on a flat bench — the best-case
geometry for this family. Note the honest counterweight from `Deng2021vector`: equivariance
costs in-distribution accuracy (VN-PointNet 77.5 vs 85.9). The gain is a **low-data** gain;
`Wang2024equivariant` itself shows the advantage compressing at 1000 demos.

### I3 — Perception-error tolerance curve

*Cheapest experiment here, and the one that makes both halves of the team necessary.*

Feed the policy an object pose corrupted by `Exp(ξ)`, `ξ ~ N(0, Σ)`, sweeping σ across the
CV team's **already-measured** error budget from `computer-vision/labvision/scene.py`:

| Anchor | Measured placement error |
| --- | --- |
| `fit` | 0.00 mm against the synthetic oracle |
| `base`, with radius correction | 3–8 mm |
| `base`, without radius correction | 23–59 mm |

Output: **success rate vs σ**. This requires zero CV runtime and hands Nacho and Martí a
precision target instead of a guess — it says which anchor mode the policy actually needs.

`03_aplicaciones_vision_por_computador.md` §6.5 notes this is also the *geometrically
correct* form of camera domain randomization: perturb the camera pose as `Exp(ξ)` with
`ξ ~ N(0, Σ_calib)`, which models hand-eye calibration error rather than arbitrary jitter.

### I4 — Barcode-conditioned identity

*The demo.*

"Place `PWD-0042` on the balance", with distractor bottles present. Two conditions:

- **(a)** language-only conditioning on a from-scratch policy;
- **(b)** identity token produced by `computer-vision/labvision/reader.py` from a wrist crop.

This is SmolVLA's slot in the plan. `04_huecos_y_ampliacion.md` §H7 frames the label as a
**symmetry breaker**: the bottles are near-SO(2)-symmetric, and the barcode fixes the angle
about the vertical axis — it should be treated as an extra observation, not as noise.

> **Hard constraints, now measured in the rendered scene.** `computer-vision/scripts/wrist_scan.py`
> flies the scene's `wrist` GoPro at one bottle after another and decodes the whole 1080p
> frame with nothing telling the reader where the label is: **19 of 20 read, none misread**.
> The fixed `general` camera, 2.5 m and more from every label, decodes **none** — "the fixed
> camera proposes, the wrist camera confirms".
>
> Working distance is a function of vessel size, and it sets where the policy must bring the
> camera:
>
> | Bottle | Reads from |
> | --- | --- |
> | Powder 2 L / 500 ml / 250 ml | 0.30 m |
> | Powder 1 L, Liquid 100 ml | 0.30 m sometimes, else 0.20 m |
> | Powder 100 ml | 0.20 m |
> | Liquid 50 / 30 / 20 ml | 0.15 m |
> | Liquid 10 ml | 0.10 m on the bench; **not at all** at the back of a shelf |
>
> Three consequences for the task design:
>
> - **The small flasks need the arm closer than a GoPro focuses.** 0.10–0.15 m for the
>   10–30 ml range. The render has no defocus; a real camera at that range does. Either the
>   wrist camera gets a close-focus lens or those labels get a denser symbology — otherwise
>   I4 results on small flasks will not survive sim-to-real.
> - **Approach elevation is bounded.** Ladder-label bars are rings round the bottle, so from
>   above a ring is an arc: at **25° they bend too far for the localiser to rectify; at 8°
>   they still read**. A shelf lip hides the first digits when viewed from level, so the
>   usable window is narrow — roughly 8° above horizontal.
> - **Restrict I4 to vessels ≥ 100 ml for the first pass.** The 10–30 ml flasks carry both
>   the focus problem and the shelf-occlusion problem at once.

Related precedent, the vault's only citable one: LAPP (`Wolf2021robotic`) pairs a fiducial
for pose with a barcode for identity in a pharmaceutical lab.

### I5 — RL refinement (stretch)

Pretrain with ACT or Diffusion Policy, then refine online. Isaac Lab's parallel envs are the
whole reason this is affordable.

- **Method**: ReinFlow (`Zhang2025reinflow`) — reports **−62.8 % wall time vs DPPO**, exact
  likelihood at 1–4 denoising steps. DPPO (`Ren2024diffusion`) as the fallback.
- **Anchoring** — `04` §H4 prescribes *two* geometries at once: the trust-region KL to the
  current iterate (PPO/MDPO) **plus** an anchor to the base model, either
  `β · KL(π_θ ‖ π_base)` in policy space or an EWC penalty with a diagonal Fisher estimated
  on the demonstrations. The second is "free during SFT and does not require sampling the
  base policy".
- **Motivation**: AutoBio's authors point at RL themselves — *"compounding errors in
  screwing and unscrewing highlight the need to explore reinforcement learning for
  closed-loop performance."*

Precedent for how large the gain can be: SimpleVLA-RL took one-trajectory SFT from
**48.9 % → 96.9 %**; πRL took few-shot π0 from **57.6 % → 96.1 %**, above full-data SFT.

## 6. Evaluation protocol

Lift directly from `03_aplicaciones_vision_por_computador.md` §8.4 and
`02_optimizacion_y_algoritmos.md` §F.9:

| Level | Metric |
| --- | --- |
| Task | Success rate, **N ≥ 50 episodes with different seeds** |
| Task | Robustness: success **as a function of randomization** (lighting, texture, calibration error Σ) |
| Policy | Smoothness: jerk, and geodesic length of the end-effector SE(3) trajectory |
| Efficiency | **Success vs number of demonstrations** (data-efficiency curves) |
| Training | KL per iteration w.r.t. the imitation policy — must stay bounded |
| Safety | `σ_min(J_b)` along the trajectory, to detect proximity to singularities |

One methodological point from `04` §H7, worth honouring in the demo: ORGANA and ASHE show
that real systems depend on **closed-loop error detection and recovery** more than on a
perfect single-shot pose. ASHE reports **98.5 %** correct placement on first attempt over
130 trials *and* 10/10 injected failures corrected on the second attempt. Reporting
**recovery-aware episode success** is more convincing than reporting a single-shot metric.
